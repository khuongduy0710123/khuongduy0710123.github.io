Uncovering The Drivers of Customer Churn

A churn analysis built on real telecom customer data: a logistic regression model to identify what predicts churn, paired with an interactive Power BI dashboard for exploring who is at risk.

From signal to decision The analysis is designed to help a retention team decide where to look, what to test, and who not to discount by default.

01 / framing

The question

Knowing how many customers churned is table stakes. The useful question is where a retention team should spend its next hour, and which intervention has a credible reason to work.

01

Who needs a conversation first?

Find the customer groups where churn risk and account value overlap, rather than sorting by risk alone.

02

Which combinations are hardest to keep?

Compare contract type, internet service and protection add-ons together to surface high-risk combinations.

03

Where is churn value concentrated?

Use revenue and customer filters to see whether the commercial exposure sits in one segment or across the book.

04

Which segments justify retention investment?

Prioritise segments where a service intervention or contract conversation is more credible than a blanket discount.

05

Does revenue alone explain risk?

Test whether higher-value accounts behave differently, then read revenue alongside contract and service context.

Read the signal, not just the number.

An odds ratio above 1 indicates higher odds of churn relative to the reference category. Month-to-month contracts are the strongest signal in this set.

The service signals matter too: customers without online security or premium support, fiber optic customers and Offer E all sit above the reference point.

These are associations in the observed data, not proof that a contract or service choice causes churn. They are a basis for prioritisation and testing.

07 / implication

Key finding

The most useful output is not a list of reasons customers leave. It is a better order for the next retention conversation.

Contract type outweighs every other factor in the model: month-to-month customers show much higher churn odds than annual subscribers, regardless of how much they spend.

  • Month-to-month fiber and DSL segments rise first because contract risk and value exposure overlap.
  • Missing security and premium support are intervention hypotheses, not diagnoses to apply account by account.
  • Fiber optic and Offer E deserve deeper dashboard exploration alongside contract context rather than isolated conclusions.
  • Lower-risk, committed customers create a useful comparison group for measuring a retention test.
