Supplier Delivery Performance Review

On-time-in-full (OTIF) analysis of inbound raw materials for a lubricants manufacturer in Southeast Asia

Prepared byDanny Bui
Data2,061 purchase order lines across 612 purchase orders, 39 suppliers and 263 materials
PeriodDeliveries due January to August 2026
Inbound valueUS$91.4 million
ToolsExcel, Power Query, Power BI, DAX

1Executive summary

This review measures how reliably suppliers commit to the delivery dates and quantities requested by the purchasing team. Performance is below the 95% OTIF target on every measure, and the gap is considerably larger when purchase order lines are weighted by value.

Key findings

  1. Line-based OTIF of 72.8% overstates performance. Weighted by value, OTIF is 47.2%, because high-value base oil lines fail more often than low-value additive lines.
  2. Risk is concentrated. Two base oil suppliers account for 5% of lines, 47% of spend and 74% of value at risk.
  3. Failures are driven by timing rather than quantity. 89% of failed lines missed the date window, and most were late by 8 to 14 days.
  4. Lines requested at shorter notice than the standard lead time achieved 56% OTIF, compared with 84% for lines requested in line with it.

2Background and scope

Lubricant production depends on two main inputs: base oil, which makes up most of the blend by volume and value, and additive packages. Packaging and a small volume of traded finished goods are also purchased. A late base oil delivery can delay the blending schedule and, in turn, customer orders.

The purchasing team manages around 40 suppliers in Singapore, Malaysia, China, Korea, Japan and Europe. Before this project, delivery performance was checked supplier by supplier in the ERP system, with no agreed KPI and no consolidated view across suppliers or months.

The scope covers all purchase order lines with a requested delivery date between January and August 2026. Each line is assessed once, against the supplier's confirmed ETA and confirmed quantity.

4Methodology and KPI definitions

The source data is a purchase order line export from the ERP system. It was cleaned and prepared in Excel and Power Query, where a status flag (1 or 0) was calculated for each KPI on every line. The Power BI measures aggregate these flags, so each KPI is defined in one place.

Table 3. KPI definitions
KPIDefinition
On time (OTD)Confirmed ETA within ±7 days of the requested date
In fullConfirmed quantity within ±5% of the ordered quantity
OTIFBoth conditions met on the same line; a line without a confirmed ETA counts as a failure
OTIF (US$)Value of OTIF lines divided by total value
Value at riskTotal value of lines that failed OTIF
Lead-time complianceRequested date compared with PO date plus the material's standard lead time (SLA days), with ±7 days tolerance

Performance is measured at purchase order line level, because a single order can contain several materials with different dates.

5Findings

5.1Line-based vs value-weighted OTIF

1,501 of 2,061 lines met OTIF, a rate of 72.8%. Measured by value, the rate is 47.2%. The two measures were close in January, but they diverged from March onwards, as the value-weighted rate fell to around 40% while the line-based rate stayed mostly in the 70s.

The difference is explained by the mix of lines. Additive suppliers issue many small, mostly reliable lines, which keeps the line count high. Base oil lines are fewer but larger, averaging about US$125,000 against US$20,000 for additives, and they fail more often.

Finished goods show the lowest OTIF, but all 44 lines without a confirmed ETA come from one freight forwarder in this category, which points to a data entry issue rather than a supplier issue.

5.2Supplier concentration of risk

Ranking suppliers by value at risk shows a highly concentrated pattern. Supplier A delivered 39 lines worth US$23.7M, of which only 5 met OTIF. Supplier B reached 53% OTIF on US$19.0M of spend. Together they account for US$35.6M, or 74%, of total value at risk.

5.3Delivery delay and lead time

Quantity performance is not a material issue: 96.0% of lines were confirmed in full. Of the 560 lines that failed OTIF, 500 failed on timing, 44 had no confirmed ETA and 16 failed on quantity alone.

Most delays are moderate. 64% of late lines were 8 to 14 days beyond the requested date, which is just outside the ±7 day tolerance.

Requested dates were also compared with each material's standard lead time. 424 lines (21%) were requested more than seven days earlier than the standard lead time allows. These lines achieved 55.9% OTIF, compared with 83.6% for lines requested within the standard lead time. Part of the measured delay therefore originates in internal planning rather than supplier performance.

8Data model and DAX measures

The model is a star schema with one fact table at PO line grain and four dimension tables. All relationships are many-to-one with single-direction filtering. The requested ETA has an inactive relationship to the date table, used for analysis by delivery month.

Core measures:

// Share of PO lines on time and in full
OTIF = DIVIDE ( SUM ( FactPOLine[OTIFFlag] ), [Lines] )

// OTIF weighted by line value
OTIF(USD) =
DIVIDE (
    SUMX ( FactPOLine, FactPOLine[AmountUSD] * FactPOLine[OTIFFlag] ),
    [Value USD]
)

// Value of lines failing OTIF
Value at Risk (USD) =
CALCULATE ( [Value USD], FactPOLine[OTIFFlag] = 0 )

// Supplier ranking that respects report filters
Vendor Rank by Value at Risk =
RANKX ( ALLSELECTED ( DimVendor[VendorName] ), [Value at Risk (USD)], , DESC, DENSE )

The model also includes month-on-month change measures for OTD, In Full and OTIF using DATEADD, and an OTIF target with a gap-to-target measure.

9Limitations

  • On-time status is based on the supplier's confirmed ETA, not the goods receipt date. The measure shows whether suppliers commit to requested dates; actual arrival may differ.
  • The period covers eight months of requested delivery dates. Some months contain only a few large base oil lines, so the value-weighted rate is sensitive to individual shipments.
  • Supplier names are anonymised in this write-up. The embedded report is the working version.
